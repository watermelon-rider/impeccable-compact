# Craft floor

Load this immediately before a UI edit. The brief or committed visual world wins
unless a rule is an explicit ban. The detector handles mechanical duplication
when it is available; this file covers checks that require judgment.

## Verify in one render pass

- **Contrast:** body and placeholder text at least 4.5:1; large text at least
  3:1. Secondary text on a colored surface must remain related to that surface,
  not become neutral gray by reflex.
- **Hierarchy:** the first viewport has one dominant idea and an obvious next
  step. Headings, body, navigation, and actions agree about priority.
- **Type:** readable measure, balanced line breaks, deliberate weight and scale
  steps, no overflow with real copy, and no display face chosen only because it
  is installed.
- **Spacing:** tight groups share proximity; sections separate clearly; repeated
  components use a stable rhythm.
- **Color and depth:** every role has a job. Shadows have offset and blur.
  Zero-offset colored halos are decoration, not depth.
- **Motion:** one authored moment is better than effects on every section.
  Motion must not block reading, focus, or task completion. Respect reduced
  motion.
- **States:** hover, focus, active, disabled, loading, empty, error, and success
  are complete wherever they materially apply.
- **Browser surfaces:** focus rings, selection, caret, scrollbars, underlines,
  and numeric alignment belong to the design too.
- **Copy:** labels name actions; errors name both problem and recovery; claims
  come from supplied truth or are honestly labeled.
- **Coverage:** every requirement in the brief is present and findable.

## Refuse category defaults

These are not universal prohibitions when the brief explicitly earns them.
Treat them as evidence that no decision was made.

- same-size icon, heading, and paragraph card grids as the page structure
- nested cards
- hero metric templates with a large number and supporting stat strip
- kickers or eyebrow labels above headings
- numbered section markers that carry no real sequence
- gradient text
- glass and blur used as generic decoration
- colored side-stripe borders on cards, alerts, or list items
- hard offset shadows outside a world that explicitly uses that material
- decorative rings, sparklines, or rounded rectangles standing in for content
- monospace used as a costume for technical
- emoji or Unicode glyphs as the icon system when icons are required
- icon tiles, stock gradients, or generic geometric masks used in place of
  authored content
- modal dialogs for tasks that do not need interruption or protected focus

When a default appears because the brief chose it, keep it and make the choice
specific. When the axis was free, replace the element rather than softening it.
